Dog kiwi guide: preparation, portions, and safety

# Dog kiwi guide: preparation, portions, and safety ## Is kiwi safe for dogs? Yes. Kiwi is non-toxic for dogs. It contains actinidain, an enzyme that causes mild sensitivity in some dogs, but the fruit itself is safe. Most dogs eat kiwi without any reaction. ## How to prepare kiwi for a dog 1. Wash the kiwi 2. Peel completely. The fuzzy skin is non-toxic but hard to digest and most dogs won't eat it anyway 3. Slice into appropriate pieces based on dog size (see below) 4. First time only: give one small slice and wait 30 minutes before offering more Seeds: leave them in. Non-toxic, pass through without issue. ## Portion guide by weight | Dog size | Weight | Safe amount | |---|---|---| | Small | Under 10kg | 1-2 small slices | | Medium | 10-25kg | 2-3 slices | | Large | 25kg+ | 3-4 slices or up to half a kiwi | A few times per week. Not daily since sugar content at 9g/100g is the limiting factor. ## Green vs golden kiwi Green kiwi: more actinidain, less sugar, tart. Golden kiwi: less actinidain (better for sensitive dogs), more sugar, sweeter. First time: try golden. After that either works, just adjust portions for golden due to the higher sugar. ## Frozen kiwi 1. Peel and slice as above 2. Lay pieces flat on a tray 3. Freeze for 2+ hours 4. Transfer to a container, keep frozen 5. Serve straight from freezer Freezing partially deactivates actinidain. Better for sensitive stomachs and dogs like the cold texture. ## Signs of actinidain sensitivity Watch for: - Pawing at mouth - Drooling more than usual - Reluctance to swallow - Mild GI upset within an hour If it persists or seems severe, call the vet. Usually resolves on its own. ## When to be careful - Kidney disease (CKD): high potassium at 312mg/100g may matter on restricted diets - Calcium oxalate stone history: moderate oxalate content, check with vet - Diabetic dogs: monitor for the higher sugar load - Dogs sensitive to papaya, pineapple, or figs: higher chance of actinidain reaction ## Kiwi berries Small grape-sized fruits from the same genus. Edible skin, no peeling needed. Natural portion size for medium and large dogs. Sweeter and milder than regular kiwi. Same actinidain caveat: try one first.
